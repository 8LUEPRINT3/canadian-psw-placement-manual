# Canadian PSW Clinical Placement — Complete Manual

**Role, Scope, Legislation, and What to Expect on Placement**

## PART A — What to Expect on Placement

### Where you'll be
Personal Support Workers (PSWs) work in four main settings:
- **Long-Term Care (LTC) homes**
- **Retirement homes**
- **Hospitals** (acute care)
- **Home care / community care**

You are an **Unregulated Care Provider (UCP)**. You are not licensed by a provincial college. You always work **under the direction** of a Registered Nurse (RN) or Registered Practical Nurse (RPN), or your employer's policies and the client's **care plan**.

### Your first days
- Attend site **orientation** (fire exits, emergency codes, call-bell system, infection-control, where supplies are).
- Learn the **care plan** for each client you're assigned — never work from memory or guesswork.
- Learn who your **supervising nurse** is and how to reach them.
- Learn the **documentation system** (paper chart, electronic record) and the **progress-note format** (DARP — see Part E).

### Professional conduct (you are evaluated on this)
- **Be prepared:** know your client list, gather supplies before care.
- **Be punctual** and reliable.
- **Grooming & hygiene:** clean uniform, hair tied back, minimal jewelry, short/clean nails.
- **Confidentiality:** never discuss clients in hallways, elevators, cafeterias, or on social media (PHIPA — Part C).
- **Peer feedback:** give and receive feedback respectfully.

### The golden rules (always / never)
**ALWAYS:**
- Acknowledge the client first; make them feel heard.
- Stay in your scope; describe, don't diagnose.
- Document facts + direct quotes only (DARP).
- Report changes and abnormal findings immediately.
- Stay with a client in distress; pull the call bell.

**NEVER:**
- Never diagnose or interpret a client's condition.
- Never administer medication or perform a controlled act.
- Never change the care plan on your own.
- Never leave a client in respiratory/physical distress alone.
- Never share PHI outside the immediate care team.

## PART B — The PSW Role & Scope

### What a PSW is
A PSW is an **Unregulated Care Provider (UCP)** — not licensed or monitored by a provincial governing body. The role is **supportive and assistive**, focused on the **Activities of Daily Living (ADLs)** and **Instrumental ADLs (IADLs)**.

- **Physical personal care:** eating, bathing, dressing, grooming, toileting, skin care, oral hygiene, functional mobility.
- **Psychological, cognitive & social support:** cognitive stimulation, social engagement, dignity, self-worth, quality of life.
- **IADLs:** meal planning/preparation, light housekeeping, shopping, managing money (per care plan).
- **Philosophy:** **client-centered care** — the client as a whole person; respect autonomy, voice, self-determination, and participation in decisions.

### PSW vs RPN vs RN — the boundary lines

| Attribute | PSW (UCP) | RPN | RN |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Regulation** | Unregulated, unlicensed; no legislated scope | Regulated (CNO, RHPA) | Regulated (CNO, RHPA + Nursing Act) |
| **Education** | 5–12 month certificate | 2-year diploma | 4-year BScN degree |
| **Source of "scope"** | **Scope of Employment** (job description, care plan, agency policy) | Scope of Practice (RHPA + College standards) | Broadest Scope of Practice (incl. complex/unstable) |
| **Controlled acts** | Never initiate/perform unless delegated + trained + supervised | Yes, for stable clients | Yes, stable & unstable/complex |
| **Medication** | **Assist only** (open bottle, pour water, hand pre-dosed pack) | Administer complex/scheduled meds | Administer + IV therapy + pharmacologic monitoring |
| **Assessment** | Foundational: observe, reflect, communicate | Independent nursing assessment | Comprehensive / diagnostic |

### Three boundary rules you must never break
1. **Never diagnose or interpret.** Record "temp 101.4°F and shivering," never "client has an infection."
2. **Decline out-of-scope requests.** Inserting catheters, adjusting IV rates, prescription eye-drops, deep-wound dressings = controlled acts — decline unless formally delegated, trained, tested, and supervised.
3. **No unilateral plan revisions.** You carry out the care plan; only the RN/RPN/supervisor changes it.

## PART C — Legislation You Must Know

### 1. PHIPA (Personal Health Information Protection Act, 2004)
- Governs collection, use, and protection of **Personal Health Information (PHI)**: name, age, diagnosis, room number, observations, vital signs.
- **Share PHI only with the immediate interprofessional care team** for that client.
- Sharing with staff not on the team, discussing clients in public spaces, or posting photos/text on social media = **PHIPA violation** and serious misconduct.

### 2. Fixing Long-Term Care Act, 2021 — Resident Bill of Rights
- **Dignity & autonomy:** treated with dignity and respect; safe, clean environment.
- **Right to refuse care:** every resident can refuse any care/treatment after being informed of consequences. **Respect the choice.**

### 3. Mandatory Elder Abuse & Neglect Reporting
Under the **Fixing Long-Term Care Act (s.24(1))** and the **Retirement Homes Act, 2010 (s.75.1)**, you **must** immediately report any suspected, alleged, or witnessed abuse or neglect.

- **Consent bypass:** in LTC and retirement homes, the reporting duty **overrides client consent**. Report even if the resident begs you not to.
- **Reporting targets:**
  - **LTC homes** → Director, Ministry of Health & Long-Term Care — **1-866-434-0144**
  - **Retirement homes** → Registrar, Retirement Homes Regulatory Authority (RHRA) — **1-800-361-7254**
  - **OPG&T (Public Guardian & Trustee)** — if a POA/SDM is suspected of abuse or not acting in the client's interest — Guardianship Investigations **1-800-366-0335** (Toronto **416-327-6348**)
  - The **unit manager/designate** ensures EASI screening and formal external reports are filed.

## PART D — The 4-Step Clinical Loop (Your Operating System)

Use this for **every** clinical, ethical, or physical change on the floor:

1. **Acknowledge & Validate** — eye level, active listening, reflect feelings; never argue.
2. **Stay in Scope (No Diagnosis)** — observe with your senses; never name a cause.
3. **Document Factual Data (DARP)** — objective facts + direct quotes.
4. **Escalate & Report** — tell the RN/RPN immediately; never leave a client in distress alone.

---

## PART E — DARP Charting Standard

- **D (Data):** exact numbers (temp, BP, RR), location (oral/axillary), position, which arm, direct quotes, what you saw/heard/smelled. Write immediately — never from memory.
- **A (Action):** support given + who you notified (name, title, time).
- **R (Response):** how the client responded (quotes, pain behaviours like frowning, guarding).
- **P (Plan):** continued care/monitoring under the care plan or nurse's direction.

> Never write opinions ("difficult," "lazy") or vague language ("seems off"). These are unprofessional and a legal liability if the file is subpoenaed.

## PART F — Skills Scope Reference (In-Scope / Delegation / Controlled / Observe)

| Skill | PSW Status |
| :--- | :--- |
| Basic math & conversions (for med prep) | ✅ In scope |
| Medication **assistance** (open bottle, pour water, hand pre-dosed pack) | ✅ In scope |
| Medication **administration** | ❌ Controlled act (RPN/RN) |
| Glucometer (blood glucose) | ⚠️ Delegation only |
| Insulin pens | ❌ Controlled act (RPN/RN) |
| Safe med practice (ISMP) / error-prone abbreviations | ✅ In scope (knowledge) |
| Wound care — simple clean/dry dressing | ⚠️ Delegation only |
| Wound care — deep/complex | ❌ Controlled act (RPN/RN) |
| Hot & cold application | ✅ In scope |
| Suctioning | ❌ Controlled act (RN/RPN) |
| IV site care | 📋 Observe & report (never adjust IV) |
| Oxygen therapy | ⚠️ Delegation only (never initiate/adjust flow) |
| Tracheostomy care | ❌ Controlled act (RN/RPN) |
| Catheter insertion | ❌ Controlled act (RN/RPN); PSW observes/reports output |
| Vital signs, pain assessment, personal care, mobility, transfers, positioning, elimination care | ✅ In scope |

## PART G — The 10 Critical Scenarios (Frontline Playbook)

### Scenario 1 — Client Care Refusal (Dignity & Autonomy)
- **Situation:** Capable resident Mrs. Miller refuses her weekly tub bath: "I'm too tired… leave me alone."
- **The move:** Halt prep. Cover with a warm blanket, sit at eye level, listen. "That is completely your choice and I respect that. Would you prefer a quick sink wash, or should I check back in an hour?"
- **Why:** Resident Bill of Rights — the right to refuse; forcing care = negligence/battery/assault.
- **Escalate:** inform the nurse of the refusal.
- **DARP:** D — refusal + quote + alert/oriented. A — respected refusal, offered alternatives, call bell within reach, notified RN. R — "Thank you, dear," resting. P — re-approach in 1 hour.

### Scenario 2 — Suspected Physical Abuse in LTC (Mandatory Reporting)
- **Situation:** Bruises on Mr. Vance's thighs/arms; he flinches and whispers, "Don't tell my son."
- **The move:** Stop care. Keep him safe and covered. Don't touch the bruises. "You are safe here. I need to let the nurse know so we can keep you safe."
- **Why:** LTC Act s.24(1) — mandatory reporting; **law overrides consent.**
- **Escalate:** report to unit manager; file report to Ministry — 1-866-434-0144.
- **DARP:** D — size/location/colour of bruises + quote. A — reported to manager (name, title, time). R — held your hand, stopped whispering. P — manager initiates assessment + EASI + Ministry report.

### Scenario 3 — Suspected Financial Abuse in Retirement Home
- **Situation:** Daughter forcing Mrs. Thorne to sign over her house; threats about stopping payments.
- **The move:** Intervene calmly, stay with the resident, pause the situation, comfort her. Never leave her alone.
- **Why:** Retirement Homes Act s.75.1 — mandatory report to RHRA (1-800-361-7254); OPG&T for suspected POA abuse (1-800-366-0335).
- **Escalate:** manager + RHRA + OPG&T.
- **DARP:** D — exact quotes from both. A — intervened, notified manager, security. R — daughter left; resident stopped crying. P — monitor q30min; manager files reports.

### Scenario 4 — Suspected Neglect in Home Care
- **Situation:** Bedbound Mr. Zhao in soiled bedding, cachectic, sacral ulcer; daughter dismissive.
- **The move:** Gloves, perineal hygiene, change linens, reposition off the ulcer. Do **not** apply creams/dressings. Escalate to supervisor + Care Coordinator.
- **Why:** neglect = failure to provide necessities of life; imminent risk → safety planning.
- **DARP:** D — wound size/appearance + client/daughter quotes. A — hygiene, reposition, water, notified supervisor + wound-care referral. R — "Much better, thank you." P — reposition q2h; supervisor coordinates CCAC safety plan.

### Scenario 5 — High Temperature (Vitals Deviation)
- **Situation:** Oral temp 101.4°F (38.6°C), skin hot, shivering, achy.
- **The move:** Comfortable position; confirm with a second thermometer probe; no meds, no cold packs. Report to nurse.
- **Why:** >100.2°F = abnormal fever; older adults' baselines run low. Never give Tylenol yourself.
- **DARP:** D — temp + location + baseline + symptoms. A — semi-Fowler's, confirmed reading, reported to charge nurse. R — "I just want to rest." P — monitor; nurse assesses for orders (blood draw/antipyretics).

### Scenario 6 — Dyspnea / Respiratory Distress (Emergency)
- **Situation:** Mr. Vance gasping, RR 28, wheezing, lips cyanotic.
- **The move:** **DO NOT LEAVE HIM.** Call bell immediately. Sit him upright (high-Fowler's), loosen collar, calm reassuring voice. No oxygen/inhalers unless delegated.
- **Why:** normal RR 12–20; 28 = tachypnea + cyanosis = severe distress.
- **DARP:** D — RR + rhythm + wheeze + cyanosis. A — upright, collar loosened, call bell, stayed with client, reported. R — "Hard… to… breathe," nurse arrived with O₂. P — assist nursing, monitor under supervision.

### Scenario 7 — Severe Pain in a Non-Verbal Client (5th Vital Sign)
- **Situation:** Mrs. Rossi (advanced dementia) grimacing, groaning, clutching hip, bracing during transfer.
- **The move:** **Stop the transfer.** Comfortable supported position. Escalate for analgesia before resuming. Use FACES/PAINAD.
- **Why:** pain is the 5th vital sign; not a normal part of aging; analgesics are a controlled act.
- **DARP:** D — pain cues + FACES 8/10. A — aborted transfer, repositioned, reported to RPN. R — groaning stopped. P — await analgesic; recheck in 30 min.

### Scenario 8 — Family Demands Resident's Medical Details (PHIPA)
- **Situation:** Son demands BP numbers and lab results: "I have a right to know!"
- **The move:** Stay calm and firm. No numbers, no chart, no interpretation. "I'm not authorized to release that. I'll connect you with the charge nurse."
- **Why:** PHIPA — PHI is confidential; interpretation/release is the RN/RPN's role.
- **Escalate:** notify the charge nurse of the request.
- **DARP:** D — who asked + what + quote. A — declined, redirected to nurse, notified charge nurse. R — directed him to station. P — route future inquiries to regulated team.

### Scenario 9 — Asked to Do Out-of-Scope Dressing & Medication
- **Situation:** Busy RPN: "Change Mrs. Rossi's dressing and give her two Tylenol — you know how!"
- **The move:** Decline politely but firmly; offer in-scope help (call bells, transfers, monitoring).
- **Why:** medication administration + clinical dressing = controlled acts (RHPA/Nursing Act). No formal delegation = misconduct.
- **Escalate:** tell your clinical instructor if pressured.
- **DARP:** D — who asked + what. A — declined per CACCE/RHPA, offered alternatives. R — "Thanks for the bells." P — adhere to scope.

### Scenario 10 — Break-Room Gossip & Social-Media Selfie (Ethics & Privacy)
- **Situation:** Peer's selfie shows a resident's name/room; plans to post to Instagram; staff gossiping about the family.
- **The move:** Speak up immediately. "Don't post that — the name and room number are PHI. This is a PHIPA violation." Stop the gossip.
- **Why:** PHIPA protects images/names/room numbers; gossip violates ethics and conduct.
- **Escalate:** ensure photo deleted; report to clinical instructor.
- **DARP:** D — what was photographed + intent to publish. A — intervened, cited PHIPA, had photo deleted, reported to instructor. R — "I didn't realize… thanks." P — privacy refresher training.

## PART H — How to Practice (Simulator)

Ask the coach to **"Start Simulator"** and it will present one unscripted clinical dilemma at a time. You respond; you're scored out of **30** across three domains (10 each):

1. **Communication & Empathy** — active listening, validation, no defensiveness, dignity.
2. **Scope & Safety Adherence** — stays in UCP scope, declines out-of-scope tasks, flags abuse triggers.
3. **Documentation & Reporting** — complete factual DARP note; correct reporting targets (titles, numbers).

**Rubric:** 9–10 excellent · 7–8 proficient · 5–6 developing · 0–4 critical failure.
