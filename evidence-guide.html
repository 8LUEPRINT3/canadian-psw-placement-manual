Why good documentation matters: it's the legal record of what happened, it keeps the care team informed, it protects the client, and it protects you if the file is ever reviewed or subpoenaed.

The golden standard: DARP

Write every occurrence as a DARP note. That's the structure built into your log.

LetterMeaningWhat goes in it
DDataExact numbers (temp, BP, RR), location (oral/axillary), position, which arm, direct quotes, what you saw/heard/smelled/felt.
AActionSupport given + who you notified (exact name, title, time).
RResponseHow the client responded (quotes, pain behaviours like frowning/guarding).
PPlanContinued care / monitoring under the care plan or nurse's direction.

What makes a note stand up

DO:
  • Write immediately — never from memory. Exact numbers go down the moment you read them.
  • Facts only — objective observations, not conclusions.
  • Exact quotes — in quotation marks, word for word.
  • Times and dates — when you observed, when you acted, when you notified.
  • Who you told — full name, professional title, time of notification.
  • Direct, specific language — "3 cm × 4 cm red area on sacrum," not "sore spot."
DON'T:
  • Never diagnose or interpret — write "temp 101.4°F and shivering," never "client has an infection."
  • Never use opinions — "difficult," "lazy," "attention-seeking."
  • Never use vague language — "seems off," "doing fine."
  • Never backdate or alter a note after the fact.
  • Never leave gaps — if something happened, there should be a note.

Personal log vs. the official chart

Your private log (this tool)The official chart
PurposeYour own memory aid / study recordThe legal, clinical record
Who sees itOnly you (encrypted, local)The care team, auditors, possibly a court
What it isSupplementary, personalThe authoritative source
Best practice: log the factual occurrence in both — chart it formally at the site and keep your own timestamped copy here. Never put anything in the official chart that isn't factual and complete.

Preserving your evidence

  • Timestamp everything — your log auto-stamps each entry with a receipt ID + date/time.
  • Export regular backups — use "Backup (.json)" or "Export all (.md)" so you keep a copy even if you clear the browser.
  • Copy receipts — the "Copy receipt" button gives you a formatted, timestamped record you can paste into notes/email to yourself.
  • Keep it secure — your passcode is the only key; don't leave an unlocked device unattended.
